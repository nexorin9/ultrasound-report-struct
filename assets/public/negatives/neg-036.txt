# title: Hydatid pulmonary arterial embolization from an intracardiac cyst mimicking pulmonary thromboembolism: A case report

FINDINGS · Case presentation
A 14-year-old boy from an endemic rural region presented with progressive dyspnea, intermittent chest pain, recurrent hemoptysis, and severe resting hypoxemia, with an oxygen saturation of 85% on room air. CT pulmonary angiography demonstrated extensive bilateral pulmonary arterial filling defects with cystic morphology, associated arterial dilatation, and representative attenuation values of approximately −5 HU. Despite the extensive vascular obstruction, D-dimer was only mildly elevated at 0.83 mg/L FEU (reference range, 0–0.5 mg/L FEU), compared with 0.41 mg/L FEU during a clinically stable period. Transthoracic echocardiography identified an approximately 22 × 22 mm right-sided intracardiac cyst adjacent to the tricuspid valve, and Echinococcus IgG ELISA was positive at 32 U/L (positive threshold, >11 U/L). The patient underwent median sternotomy with cardiopulmonary bypass, intracardiac cyst excision, and bilateral pulmonary arteriotomy, with removal of 18 large pulmonary arterial cystic structures. Follow-up CT pulmonary angiography at 6 months demonstrated marked regression of the intravascular disease burden, with small residual distal lesions. However, repeat CT approximately 12 months after surgery demonstrated an approximately 15–20% visually estimated increase in bilateral pulmonary arterial cystic burden, with new proximal pulmonary arterial cystic lesions, consistent with interval radiological progression. This progression was documented after discontinuation of albendazole, although a causal relationship could not be established.

CONCLUSION · Conclusion
Pulmonary arterial hydatid embolization should be considered when presumed pulmonary thromboembolism is accompanied by discordant laboratory and imaging findings, particularly in endemic regions. In this patient, extensive bilateral fluid-attenuation intravascular lesions, only mildly elevated D-dimer despite severe obstruction, and a concomitant intracardiac cyst supported a non-thrombotic embolic process. Combined surgical and antiparasitic treatment resulted in marked initial radiological improvement; however, subsequent progression of residual pulmonary arterial disease highlights the need for prolonged radiological surveillance and careful reassessment of the duration of antiparasitic therapy.
